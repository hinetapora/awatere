What I'm building now

PayStage — the check that runs before an agent pays

In plain words: your AI assistant can now buy things on its own — a data lookup, an API call, a report. PayStage checks each purchase against the rules you set before it is paid: how much per purchase, how much a day, who it may buy from. If it doesn’t fit, it is stopped. Like a company card with a limit — except the limit is actually checked every time, and you get a receipt for every decision.

An independent, read-only preflight for x402 payments. Before an AI agent signs anything it gets back pay, review or do_not_pay, with the rule that fired — caps, velocity windows, allow-lists, and a receipt for every decision.

Open source · no custody · no keys held · built on Base · work in progress

VouchGuard — the same limits, in a form a person can hold

In plain words: AI assistants spend money — a few cents a call, hundreds of calls a day — and the first most companies hear of it is the invoice. VouchGuard puts each assistant on a budget you can actually see: what it may spend per purchase, what it may spend in a day, who it may pay, and when the permission ends. It sits in the wallet on your phone as a pass, so you can check it in a second and change it in one tap — and anyone who needs to verify it can scan it. No month-end surprise, and no dashboard to remember.

A spending mandate on an Apple Wallet pass: per-purchase cap, a per-24h ceiling, an expiry and a revoke. The person paying can see what a teammate may spend without opening a dashboard; a counterparty can scan it and read the same state.

A record of authority, not a payment card · open source under MIT · rebrandable for your own business · work in progress
